There are four characteristics that describe my father-in-law:

1. After retirement, he got a lot of free time on his hands and keeps looking for things to do.
2. He usually doesn't like to purchase new things, as he thinks they are too overpriced.
3. He doesn't like things that don't work well and has some ability to fix them.
4. Despite his talents for fixing electrical and mechanical devices (mainly his motorcycle), he doesn't know much about computers.

In recent years, there is one thing he keeps trying to fix: the satisfaction my mother-in-law has with her smartphone. He always asks me for help to get a good smartphone for her but can't quite understand what makes a smartphone good. He thinks that if it has more storage, it will be faster, and he can't really understand why a cellphone actually needs a processor.

I think people understand better when you compare a hard-to-understand situation with one that is more familiar: the famous analogy! Over these years, I have come up with some analogies to explain smartphones to him, and maybe this can help other people understand as well.

Maybe what really describes it is a motorcycle:


## Understanding the storage vs. processor

A motorcycle doesn't go faster if you double its fuel tank. It may even go slower since it's now heavier. This is the same relationship between the processor and storage. The phone will not become faster if you increase the storage. In the same way, you need a stronger motor to make a motorcycle go faster, you need a faster processor to make the cellphone faster.

## Understanding the RAM memory

What better describes RAM memory is a delivery truck. The tasks you want to do on your phone are always composed of many steps, which can be seen as many packages that must be delivered. The amount of RAM memory you have is the size of your delivery truck.
If you have only a couple of packages to be delivered, you may only need a small truck.
But if you have a lot of packages to be delivered, you either will need to make multiple trips, which will increase the time to complete the transportation (task completion), or you'll need a bigger truck.

The processor would be the person responsible for loading and unloading the truck. If you have a stronger processor, it's like having a stronger person or even a forklift. The load/unload process will be done faster.
If you have a weaker processor, it will take longer to load/unload the truck. Depending on the package weight, it may even be impossible to load or unload.

## So, what configuration do I need for my phone?

It will depend on your use and budget. The stronger the processor, and the bigger the storage and RAM memory, the more expensive the phone will be.

If the tasks you do daily are simple (only a few packages), you don't need a really big truck. This would be an intermediate phone or an old flagship phone.

In 2024, it would be the Samsung A series, the latest strong Redmi phone, or older iPhones and Galaxy S phones, up to 5 years old.

If you want speed and almost no lag when scrolling through a lot of social media posts, photos, etc., you'll need newer flagships like a 2-3 year-old iPhone, Samsung Galaxy S/Z, or top-tier phones from other brands.

Of course, these tips are for those who want to use the phone with almost no lag. But if you don't mind some lag or waiting a few more seconds to complete tasks, a phone with lower specs is reasonable.

### Other points to consider in the price

Another point to consider is the quality of the phone material. If the phone is well-built with high-quality materials, it will last longer. It's common for an iPhone to last 5-8 years in careful hands. Conversely, cheaper phones may last only a year even in careful hands. This should be factored into your price calculations. Spend $1,000 on a phone that will last really well for 4-5 years, or $300 on a phone that will last for a year. In this example, the first option is cheaper and will deliver more speed and quality in many aspects, for example, photo quality.
